Price setup reads the weekly closes since the peak (all as a share of the high): breakdown pending = a real bounce (it retraced at least 30% of the first leg down) that has rolled over at least 5% from its rebound high and now sits within 8% of the post-peak low. A weekly close below that low is the confirmation the playbooks use, and where the template's long fall begins. Rolling over = past the rebound high but still well above the low; bouncing = still near the rebound high (too early); no real bounce or still falling = the low is recent, so there is no failed rally to sell (chasing). Template phase is the post-peak fit's position: after the peak (u 0.79) the denial dip bottoms near u 0.82, the bounce ("return to normal") tops near u 0.84, the long fall runs to the trough near u 0.92. Template fall left is the share of the template's peak-to-trough drop still ahead at that position: a timing reading, not a price target (the template's trough is extreme: Nasdaq 2000, Nikkei 1989). Fits and setups can disagree; when they do, trust the price levels. Long-side watchlists are included for reference. Short potential: from top is how far price already sits below its highest weekly close; risk: up to stop is the rise to the stop: the rebound high after the post-peak low when that rebound was a real one (it retraced at least 30% of the first leg), otherwise the peak, the stop rule the playbooks use; down to long-term trend is the fall to the 200-week moving average, the trend line bubbles tend to deflate back to (fits start in Oct 2022, so it averages about four years; positive means price is already below it); down to pre-bubble base is the fall to the lowest weekly close in the two years before the peak, the template's full deflation (blank when the peak came too early in the data to have a base); reward : risk is the downside to trend divided by the risk to the stop (above 1: more room to fall to trend than to rise to the stop).
